# Sentences

Read every sentence on a page against this list before publishing. Read
the headings and the sentences that join one section to the next against
it as well. Those are written last and skimmed first, and a claim or a
piece of filler survives there more often than in the sentences that
carry the argument.

1. It has a subject and a verb, and states what a thing is or does. A
   fragment standing in for a claim ("One form.") is not a sentence.
2. It holds one idea. Two half-thoughts joined by a semicolon are two
   sentences.
3. It states the fact instead of hinting at it. "Publishing an old
   revision id does not work" hints. "`publish` refuses a revision id that
   already exists in artifacts/" states.
4. It uses the real name: the function, the file, the command, the number.
5. A heading or a label names a thing and makes no claim.
6. It has no flourish: no inverted word order, no aphorism, no "the way X
   does".
7. It says something about this plan. A sentence that could sit unchanged
   on another plan says nothing about this one; cut it.
8. A reader who has never seen the project could restate it as a fact
   about the plan.
9. It appears once. A subheading, a label or the first sentence under a
   heading does not repeat the heading.
